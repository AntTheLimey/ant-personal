# Creative Writing Patterns

## Narrative Fiction

### Opening a Story
- Drop into action or tension immediately — no preamble, no weather reports
- Establish character through physical description and motion in the first paragraph
- Sensory immersion first — smell, sound, texture — then sight, then context
- Example (from High Jingo): "The first murder was unplanned. The first promotion, unexpected."
- Example (from Collem): "Collem West, or Von West as most people still knew him, strode down the road at a strong pace. Fury was upon his brow and purpose enough in his stride to make a horse hard pressed to follow for long."
- Example (from GURPS Rome): Opens with layered sensory detail in order of dominance — "Smell is strongest...sweat, oil, leather" → vision of "backs of metal helmets" → hearing of "cough or far-away echo" — body before mind

### Character Introduction Pattern
1. Physical description — height, build, a distinguishing feature
2. Situational context — what they're doing right now
3. Personality revealed through action or reaction — not told to the reader
4. Backstory woven in naturally through context, never infodumped

Example sequence:
- Physical: "He was a tall, strong and very blond lad just north of manhood."
- Context: He's storming away from an argument with his father
- Personality: He doesn't break stride when guards approach
- Backstory: Delivered through the guards' conversation and internal thoughts

### Character Description Shortcuts
- Use animal or food comparisons for quick reads: "A sharp nose topped by eyes too close together, gave the impression of an angry chicken"
- Physical detail signals personality: "slightly pudgy, generally soft featured" = threat underestimated
- Description of clothing/gear tells social position without explaining it
- Physical paradox as entry point: use contradictions in appearance to create immediate depth ("fattest woman you ever saw...silk and painted slapstick")
- Multiple origin stories or rumours about a character add texture and mystery — let the reader wonder which is true

### Dialogue Rules
- 62% of lines are 10 words or fewer. Keep it clipped.
- Dialogue carries plot — every exchange moves something forward
- Characters speak differently from each other (Burke is verbose and clever, Collem is blunt, Steve is anxious)
- Attribution minimal — "he said" / "she asked" / action beats instead of said-bookisms
- Profanity in dialogue is character-specific and situation-appropriate
- Example of clipped exchange:
  > "Who?" Collem asked.
  > "Do you remember Phineas Brightblade?"
  > Collem pulled a face of disgust, "Oh no. Not Captain ChunderPizzle?"

### Action Sequences
- Precise and mechanical — describe the exact physical movements
- Impact has sound and sensation: "There was a crunch, dull and meaty sounding"
- Aftermath is physical: blood, positioning of bodies, breathing
- No stylized slow-motion — things happen fast because that's how violence works
- Consequences are immediate and real: severed hands, vomit, screaming
- Combat psychology: drill/mantra first, then sensory overwhelm, then focus on one specific moment of violence, then shock
- Example pattern: "Step forward, present shields. Thrust beats the swing..." → sensory chaos → "Stab under and up...warm splash...vein found...thigh" → aftermath

### Internal Monologue
- Direct thought, present tense, usually in quotes or obvious from context
- Short and decisive: "Not good." / "I'm dangerous." / "Now what?"
- Characters process events through their own lens and biases
- Burke thinks strategically, Collem thinks simply, Steve thinks in self-justification

### Scene Transitions
- Hard rule breaks (------) between scenes. No narrative bridges.
- Time jumps stated plainly at the start of a new section: "2 weeks later"
- Location changes established in the first sentence of the new scene
- POV shifts happen at scene breaks, never mid-scene

### Pacing
- Long descriptive passages do the heavy lifting for mood, setting, and character. Short sentences arrive for action beats and emotional turns, but only after the longer prose has done the work of building to them.
- Comic relief placed after tension — not during
- Multiple plotlines interwoven through alternating scene breaks
- Placeholder notes acceptable in drafts: "<FINISH TORTURE & GETTING INFO>"
- Build-up phases (siege preparations, journey logistics), then sudden reversals (psychic explosion, betrayal, ambush)

### Short Fiction Specifics
- More lyrical and introspective register than TTRPG prose — closer to "literary fiction" than "adventure story"
- Comfortable with fragmentary or sketch forms — premise and mood can be the entire point
- Uses formal structures (diary entries, letters, dramatic monologue) to create distance from difficult material
- Dark humour as a vehicle: macabre premises delivered with casual tone (e.g., MLK's decomposition diary written as complaint-form entries)
- Animistic tendency: cities, landscapes, objects given will and personality ("the city is definitely a she")
- Betrayal, abandonment, and connection to place are recurring emotional centres
- Magical/supernatural conflict rendered as formal exchange with ritualized language — incantatory, not action-movie

## Worldbuilding / TTRPG Design

### Lore Voice
- Matter-of-fact, like a well-informed traveller explaining things over a drink
- Real-world analogues stated bluntly: "best described as early british victorian"
- Parenthetical asides for colour: "(think Tattooine moisture farmers)"
- Humour in naming: Hoitie-Toities / Lowtie-Toties, BowelBasher, Captain ChunderPizzle
- Dark settings can still be funny — tone is not uniformly grim

### Setting Description Pattern
1. Big picture first (continent/nation level)
2. Political structure (who rules, how, and what's contested)
3. Cultural flavour (real-world analogue + key differences)
4. Current tension or conflict (what makes this setting a story)
5. Institutional infrastructure — economic systems, military organisation, religious hierarchy
6. Multiple competing power centres that interact and corrupt each other

### Historical Integration
- Research integrates for authenticity and constraint, not decoration
- Use correct period-specific terminology (Roman military: scutum, gladius, pilum, cornicen, optio, centurion)
- Political/social structures reflect actual historical complexity (patronage, factional politics, class dynamics)
- Real historical figures and events remixed into fantasy settings — the history provides the skeleton, the fiction adds the muscle
- Material culture (armour types, building materials, trade goods) grounds the world in physical reality

### TTRPG Mechanics Voice
- Systems described functionally — what the player does, then what happens
- Flavour text kept separate from mechanics (don't muddle them)
- Character creation is a narrative: Origin → Youth → Focus → Road → Life-Changing Event
- Flora/fauna entries are part bestiary, part field guide, with personality baked in
- Attribute names can be invented and evocative (Forbearance, Sagacity, Integrity) rather than defaulting to D&D standards
- Calculations stated explicitly — the engineering voice, not the narrative voice

### Session Narrative Design
- Structure sessions with dramatic chapter titles: "The Assault from Hell," "Carnage in the Catacombs," "Changing of the Fates"
- Pair theatrical titles with practical bullet-point scaffolding for execution
- Use cliffhangers and reversals: psychic explosions, betrayals, simultaneous crises
- Exclamation marks sparingly but effectively: "FIGHT FIGHT FIGHT"
- Balance scripted dramatic moments with room for player agency

### NPC Descriptions
- Name, title/role, one-sentence personality summary
- Distinctive physical detail or paradox — not generic appearance
- Connect to institutional role / patronage network — politically situated, not floating in isolation
- Character revealed through rumour, action, and reputation — not exposition
- Multiple origin stories or unreliable information adds texture: "she'd started most of [the rumours about herself]"
- Motivation stated or strongly implied
- Mix authentic historical names (Quintus Caecilius Metellus) with symbolic/irreverent invented ones (Skilgannon, Slaughterous Maximus, Death-Wolf)

## Poetry

### Forms & Structure
- Free verse with sporadic rhyming is most common form
- Also uses strict AABB couplets (narrative/storytelling poems like "elizajane," "Spoke to devil")
- ABCB and ABAB schemes for more structured pieces
- Narrative verse with dialogue and character — some poems are stories
- Dramatic variation in line length: short punchy lines alternate with longer elaborate constructions
- No consistent stanza length — poems shift internal pacing frequently
- Block paragraphs of prose-like structure in longer pieces
- Whitespace used strategically for breath and emphasis
- Internal section breaks for longer poems
- Irregular meter — reads like speech rhythm, not iambic pentameter

### Thematic Range
- **Desire and passion** (erotic, romantic, spiritual) — frank, sustained, unashamed. This is a major vein. Erotic poetry uses specific sensory detail, builds from touch to climax to post-coital tenderness.
- **Internal struggle / psychological conflict** — twin selves, contradictory impulses, the war between what you want and what you are
- **Defiance and refusal** — saying "no" emphatically, refusing the devil, rejecting easy answers and false comfort. "Yeah right, shit no, and fuck you!" as repeated refrain.
- **The struggle to express** — metatextual poems about writing, the muse, the failure of language. "The page grins at me with supine smugness."
- **Dissolution, loss, and void** — absence as a presence, emptiness as weight
- **Identity and self-awareness** — consumption, modernity, who am I, what defines me
- **Mystical/supernatural encounters** — conversations with devils, demons, angry gods. Often structured as dramatic confrontations.
- **Containment vs. freedom** — corrals, fences, hounds vs. wraiths, tricks of light, escape

### Voice Signatures
- **Visceral imagery:** Blood, fire, tearing, flesh, heat — the body is always present. "Flayed apart," "liquefying flesh," "warm splash"
- **Compound constructions:** "hot-metal ire," "blood-red shreds," "vice-lock clamp," "vine-fleshed thoughts," "puppy-worry gnawing"
- **Archaic/elevated diction** inside modern phrasing: "verdance," "hoarfrost," "inchoate," "fey," "fetid," "bilious"
- **Profanity as punctuation** at emotional peaks — earned, deployed at the break point
- **"And yet"** as a pivot between contradictory states — sometimes tripled: "and yet and yet and yet"
- **Sensory immersion** — taste, touch, temperature, motion, not just visual description
- **Self-aware narrator** observing their own difference from the crowd, their own madness, their own creative struggle
- **Drooped subjects/ellipsis** for effect: "Sitting with an unkind mind / colouring others mannerisms"
- **Stream-of-consciousness** in introspective passages — fragments building on fragments

### Rhetorical Devices
- Repetition as structure — refrains that evolve through the poem, repeated openings that accumulate force
- Apostrophe — addressing the devil, the muse, the beloved, the self directly
- Questions, refusals, commands — not just declarative statements
- Lists with escalating intensity, sometimes ending with a twist or reversal
- Duality and contradiction held simultaneously — fire that both warms and burns, desire that causes pain, anger that sustains

### Tone Range
- Raw intensity/passion (sustained erotic or romantic charge)
- Defiant/confrontational (rejecting submission, laughing at the devil)
- Despairing/mournful (loss, dissolution, void)
- Playful/satirical ("Super-Power Consumer Man," "elizajane," "Schizophrenic's Goodbye")
- Introspective/meditative (watching the self, questioning language, sitting with uncertainty)
- Angry/bitter (earned, specific, not whiny)
- Mystical/visionary ("sights beyond seeing, sounds beyond hearing")

### Capitalization & Formatting
- Selective capitalization for emphasis — words get promoted to signal weight
- Title-case for mythological/spiritual entities: "Mistress of Pleasure, Goddess of Pain"
- Lowercase "i" in intimate/personal writing — deliberate vulnerability
- Internal capitalization breaks convention for compound inventions: "Vine-Fleshed," "Power-PC user"

### What Poetry Is NOT
- Not confessional in a self-pitying way — even painful poems have defiance or wit
- Not rigidly formal — structure serves feeling, not the other way around
- Not decorative — every image earns its place
- Not generic — specificity of detail is what makes it hit
