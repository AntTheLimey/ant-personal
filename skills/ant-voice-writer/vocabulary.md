# Vocabulary & Terminology

## Professional Context

### Preferred Terms
- "distributed-ready" (not "distributed-capable" or "scalable")
- "multi-master replication" (not "multi-primary" — pgEdge uses "multi-master")
- "BYOA/BYOC" for existing cloud models
- "subscription model" for the new hosted offering
- "adoption ladder" for the customer journey framework
- "supporting services" for non-database services deployed alongside databases
- "control plane" vs "cloud layer" — distinct concepts, don't conflate
- "brownfield" for existing customer acquisition strategy

### Phrases Ant Actually Uses
- "making it easy to sign up databases, not developers"
- "I do not want to get prescriptive"
- "accept technical debt to move fast"
- "stupid product manager question, but..."
- "what does this look like from the customer's perspective?"
- "connect → observe → optimize → automate → modernize → migrate"
- "the separation of concerns between X and Y"
- "here's what I'm thinking — tell me if I'm off base"

### Punctuation: Hard Ban
- **NEVER use em-dashes (—).** This is absolute. Use commas, periods, parentheses, ellipsis, or restructure the sentence. Ant does not use em-dashes in any context, ever.
- Never use semicolons.

### Words to Avoid in Professional Writing
- "synergy" / "leverage" (as verbs) / "paradigm shift"
- "best-in-class" / "world-class" / "enterprise-grade" (without proof)
- "stakeholder alignment" / "circle back" / "take offline"
- "utilize" (use "use")
- "ensure" (use "make sure" or be specific)
- "in order to" (use "to")
- Excessive hedging: "perhaps maybe we could potentially consider..."

### Question Patterns
- Asks direct questions to surface gaps: "Do we have unit costs for...?"
- Frames unknowns as open questions in documents: "Open Question: How do we..."
- Self-qualifying with humour: "stupid product manager question"
- Validates assumptions before building on them: "am I right that...?"

### Technical Comfort Level
- Understands architecture, APIs, and deployment models at a product level
- Does not write code — explicitly "not a developer"
- Comfortable with: SQL, REST APIs, Docker concepts, cloud infrastructure, PostgreSQL extensions
- Defers to engineering on: implementation detail, code review, performance tuning

## Academic / Essay Context

### Analytical Vocabulary
Ant reaches for systems-level and philosophical language when writing analytically:
- **Systems words:** perpetuated, mediated, gestalt, filter, commodified, inculcated, propagated
- **Philosophical terms used confidently:** dualism, phenomenological, commodity fetishism, dialectic, hegemonic
- **Appearance/depth language:** tangible/intangible, surface/depth, veneer, lacquered, facade
- **Pattern words:** paradox, contradiction, tension, synthesis, interplay

### Rhetorical Phrases
- "The difficulty here is..."
- "This essay will attempt..."
- "What we see is not X but Y"
- "both X and Y, neither fully X nor fully Y"
- "It would be easy to assume... but..."
- "The question is not whether... but how..."

### Words to Avoid in Academic Writing
- Empty intensifiers: "very," "really," "extremely" (be specific instead)
- Throat-clearing: "It is important to note that..." (just note it)
- Conclusion clichés: "In conclusion," "This essay has shown," "To summarize"
- False certainty: "clearly," "obviously," "undeniably" (if it were obvious, you wouldn't need to say so)

## Creative Context

### Action & Physical Vocabulary
Ant defaults to physical, concrete language. Preferred verbs:
- Movement: strode, stormed, stumbled, staggered, tumbled, scrambled, sprinted
- Impact: cracked, crunched, thumped, slammed, crashed, bounced, skittered
- Bodily: gasped, retched, vomited, flinched, shuddered, sweated, flushed
- Emotional: fumed, snarled, sputtered, beamed, grinned, squinted

### Descriptive Shortcuts
- Animal comparisons for faces/manner: "impression of an angry chicken"
- Food/drink metaphors for states: "the alcohol had turned sour in his stomach"
- Weather/light for mood: "like the sun breaking across the land"
- Sound for impact: "dull and meaty sounding"

### Poetry-Specific Vocabulary
Ant's poetry draws on a distinctive word palette:
- **Visceral/body:** flayed, splayed, eviscerate, dismember, liquefying, squelching, plunge, shuddering
- **Fire/heat:** blazing, burning, embers, ashes, scorched, searing, smouldering
- **Void/absence:** hollow, void, emptiness, dissolution, drained, husk
- **Compound inventions:** hot-metal ire, vine-fleshed thoughts, blood-red shreds, vice-lock clamp, puppy-worry gnawing, soul-beer
- **Elevated/archaic (used deliberately):** verdance, hoarfrost, inchoate, fey, fetid, bilious, mortise, venom
- **Repeated pivot words:** "and yet," "still," "but no"

### Naming Conventions (TTRPG/Fantasy)
- Mix of real-world historical names and invented ones
- Nicknames are irreverent and often crude: Captain ChunderPizzle, Bozo (for HoJo)
- Noble families use Latin/Germanic patterns: Von West, Von Kent, Palatina, Suburana
- Roman-influenced names for empire settings: Sextus Florios Linus, Gauis Fabius Maximus, Quintus Caecilius Metellus
- Celtic/Gaelic names for tribal settings: Mael Sechlain, Dyffeth, Faelan Logaire
- Symbolic/evocative names for key figures: Skilgannon, Slaughterous Maximus, Death-Wolf

### Profanity Usage Guide
- Fiction: Deployed at emotional peaks. Characters swear when angry, scared, or relieved. Not ambient.
- Poetry: Deployed at the break point — after tension builds, profanity marks the crack. "Yeah right, shit no, and fuck you!"
- Professional: Very rare. Occasionally in casual internal comms. Never in external docs.
- Personal/informal: More frequent, used for emphasis and punctuation in the way some people use exclamation marks.
- Pattern: Build tension → character/speaker breaks → profanity marks the break
- Preferred words: shit, hell, damn (common), fuck (escalation), stronger terms reserved for extreme character moments

### Humour Patterns
- Dry understatement in serious situations: "at least I still have all my appendages"
- Irreverent naming: official titles next to absurd nicknames
- Characters maintaining priorities during chaos: being hungry during a crisis
- Wry narrator observations: "This went on for a good hour before he eventually fell back on the couch"
- Self-aware internal monologue: "You have to have goals, otherwise, you're just wasting your day"
- Formal structure for absurd content: a breakup letter addressed to yourself, a decomposition diary written as customer complaints
- Wit used to handle vulnerability — depression disguised as comedy, pain wrapped in irony

### Personal/Informal Voice Markers
- Lowercase "i" in intimate or stream-of-consciousness writing
- Opens introspective passages with "i don't know..." to signal honest uncertainty
- Repeats key words for emphasis: "glory/glorying," "fire/burning," "torment/agony"
- Parenthetical asides to show thinking-aloud
- Mixes formal constructions with suddenly colloquial phrasing
- Paradox and oxymoron in philosophical/political commentary
- Stream-of-consciousness phrasing in journal-like writing — fragments building on fragments
